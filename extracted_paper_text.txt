1. The problem, in plain language 
You	want	to	price	an	option	whose	value	depends	on	many	correlated	underlyings	(a	
basket,	a	multi-factor	volatility	model,	a	multi-curve	rate	product).	Closed-form	and	grid-
based	(PDE)	methods	break	down	as	dimension	grows,	so	you	fall	back	on	Monte	Carlo.	
Monte	Carlo’s	weakness	is	that	its	error	shrinks	only	like	 	in	the	number	of	paths		—	
cutting	the	error	in	half	costs	four	times	the	compute.	
Control	variates	are	the	standard	Jix.	The	idea:	
1. You	have	a	hard,	expensive-to-simulate	payoff	 ,	whose	price	
	you	want.	
2. You	construct	a	cheaper ,	related	payoff		whose	mean	 	you	know	exactly	
(no	simulation	noise)	—	here,	from	a	reduced-dimensional	surrogate	model	.	
3. On	every	simulated	path,	you	evaluate	both		and	,	using	the	same	random	
draws.	
4. Because	’s	true	mean	is	known,	the	deviation	of	the	simulated	-average	from		
tells	you	exactly	how	“lucky”	or	“unlucky”	this	particular	batch	of	random	numbers	
was.	You	subtract	a	scaled	version	of	that	deviation	from	your	-estimate.
